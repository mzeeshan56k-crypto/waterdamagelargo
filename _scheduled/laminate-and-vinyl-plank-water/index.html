Laminate and Vinyl Plank After Water: What Survives

Laminate and vinyl plank look similar on a Largo floor and behave completely differently when water gets under them. Laminate has a fibreboard core that swells permanently. Vinyl plank is waterproof as a material, which creates a different problem entirely.

In both cases the flooring is only half the question. What sits underneath, and whether it can dry with the floor still down, decides the job.

Why Laminate Rarely Survives

The fibreboard core absorbs water and expands. The expansion is permanent, so even a floor that dries out keeps swollen edges and lifted joints.

Damage shows first at the plank ends and along seams, where the core is exposed under the wear layer. Once those edges have risen, sanding or weighting does not bring them back.

Why Waterproof Flooring Is Not a Waterproof Floor

Vinyl plank does not absorb water, and that is exactly the problem. Water that gets under it cannot evaporate upward through the plank, so it sits on the slab or subfloor underneath.

On a Largo slab, that trapped water spreads sideways and wicks into wall bases while the floor surface looks perfectly normal. Owners often discover it through a smell rather than a sight.

Click lock vinyl can sometimes be lifted carefully, the slab dried, and the same planks reinstalled. Glue down vinyl usually comes up in pieces and is replaced.

How Crews Decide

  • Meter readings at the slab or subfloor, not just the surface
  • Whether the floor is click lock or glue down
  • How long the water sat before extraction
  • Whether the water was clean or contaminated
  • Whether matching replacement material is still available

Contaminated water ends the discussion. Category three water under any floating floor means removal, because the assembly cannot be cleaned in place.

What Drying Looks Like Under a Floating Floor

Small sections are lifted at the perimeter to give air access to the subfloor. In some cases planks are removed in a strip along one wall, the assembly is dried, then the strip is reinstalled.

Where the floor cannot be lifted without destroying it, the choice is between removing it and leaving moisture in the building. Leaving it is how mold starts under a floor that still looks new.

Replacing Like for Like

Discontinued patterns are a frequent complication. Insurers generally pay for a reasonable match rather than replacing undamaged flooring in adjacent rooms, which is worth raising with your adjuster early if the floor runs continuously through the house.

Questions

Laminate and Vinyl Plank Water Questions

Can laminate flooring be saved after water damage?

Rarely. The fibreboard core swells permanently once wet, and swollen edges and lifted joints do not return to shape as the floor dries.

Is vinyl plank waterproof?

The plank material is, but the floor is not. Water gets under it through seams and edges and then cannot evaporate upward, so it sits on the slab.

Can vinyl plank be lifted and reused?

Click lock plank often can, if it is removed carefully and the slab underneath is dried. Glue down vinyl usually comes up in pieces.

How do I know if water got under my floating floor?

Meter readings at the perimeter and along seams, plus a musty smell or soft spots. Surface appearance is not a reliable guide.

What happens if water is left under vinyl plank?

It spreads along the slab and wicks into wall bases. Microbial growth follows, which is why floors that look new can still smell.

Does engineered wood survive water better than laminate?

Only slightly. Once water reaches the plywood core the layers delaminate, and delaminated plank does not recover.

Will my insurer replace the whole floor?

Usually they pay for a reasonable match in the affected area rather than replacing undamaged flooring elsewhere. Raise continuity with your adjuster early.

How long before water under a floor causes problems?

In Florida humidity, microbial growth can begin on wet porous material within about two days, so early extraction matters more than the volume of water.

Can a dehumidifier dry a floor without lifting it?

Not usually. Water trapped under a non permeable plank has no path upward, so sections normally have to be lifted to give air access.

Should I replace with something more water tolerant?

Tile over slab and true waterproof plank handle spills better, though any floating floor still traps water underneath if it gets in.
