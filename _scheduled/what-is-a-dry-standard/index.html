Drying science

What a Dry Standard Is and How It Is Set

A dry standard is the moisture reading a water damage job has to reach before equipment comes out. It is taken from the same material in an unaffected part of your building, which makes it specific to your house rather than a number copied from a chart.

Without one, drying ends when somebody decides the room feels dry. That is the single most common reason a repaired room smells six weeks later.

How the Standard Is Established

On the first visit, readings are taken from material of the same type in a part of the building the water never reached. Drywall is compared to drywall, subfloor to subfloor, framing to framing.

Those readings become the target. The wet areas are dried until they match, which accounts for the natural moisture a building carries in this climate.

MaterialWhere the baseline is taken
DrywallSame wall type in a dry room, at the same height
Subfloor or slabAn unaffected area of the same floor assembly
Framing and bottom plateDry framing in the same structure
HardwoodBoards from the same floor outside the wet zone

Why a Fixed Number Does Not Work

A 1960s concrete block house in Largo and a 2015 frame house do not carry the same baseline moisture, and neither matches a textbook figure.

Florida changes this further. Materials here sit at higher equilibrium moisture content than the same materials in a dry climate, so a number that signals a problem elsewhere can be entirely normal in Pinellas County.

What Gets Recorded

  • Baseline readings by material on day one
  • Readings from each affected area, in the same spots, every visit
  • Temperature and relative humidity inside the drying chamber
  • Grain depression across the dehumidifier
  • Equipment counts and placement changes

That record is also the document your adjuster evaluates. Daily readings showing progress toward a baseline carry more weight than a description of how wet it was.

When the Readings Stall

If material stops improving, something is wrong with the plan rather than with the building. Common causes are a cavity that air is not reaching, a source that was never fully stopped, or material too saturated to recover.

At that point the options are to change equipment placement, open a cavity for direct access, or remove the material. Running the same setup for more days rarely helps.

How the Job Closes

Final readings are taken at the same points used throughout, and they have to match the baseline. Equipment is then removed and a completion record is issued showing the readings on the final day.

Ask for that record. It is what proves the structure was dry before anything was closed up, and it is the document you want if a question arises later.

Questions

Dry Standard Questions From Largo Owners

What is a dry standard?

A moisture reading taken from unaffected material in your own building. Wet areas are dried until they match it, which makes the target specific to your house.

Why not use a fixed moisture percentage?

Because buildings differ. A 1960s block home and a new frame home carry different baseline moisture, and Florida materials sit higher than the same materials elsewhere.

Who decides when drying is finished?

The readings do. The job closes when the affected materials match the baseline taken from unaffected material of the same type.

How often are readings taken?

Every visit, usually daily, from the same points each time so progress can be compared rather than estimated.

What if the readings stop improving?

That means the plan needs changing, not more days. Usually a cavity is not getting air, the source was not fully stopped, or material is too saturated to recover.

Should I get a copy of the readings?

Yes. The log showing baseline, daily readings and final figures is what proves the structure was dry before it was closed up.

Can a room feel dry but fail the standard?

Frequently. Surfaces dry first while the wall base, cavity insulation and subfloor still hold water a meter can detect.

Does the standard change between materials?

Yes. Drywall is compared to drywall, subfloor to subfloor and framing to framing, because each material holds moisture differently.

Does my adjuster need the dry standard?

It helps. A log showing daily movement toward a baseline is stronger evidence than a description of the damage.

How long until materials reach the standard?

Most residential losses take three to five days. Block walls, tile over slab and hardwood commonly take longer.
