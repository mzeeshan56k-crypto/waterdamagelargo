Storm and Hurricane Damage Repair in Largo, FL

Storm and hurricane damage repair in Largo, FL usually starts with water that came in through the building envelope. A lifted shingle, a failed soffit, a window seal that gave up in 70 mph gusts, and suddenly rain is running down the inside of a wall. The opening has to be closed before drying can mean anything.

Pinellas County sees the full range: summer thunderstorm downbursts, tropical storm rainfall, and hurricane wind and surge. Our crews stabilize, dry and rebuild, and we document what the water did so your carrier can separate wind-driven rain from flood.

What the work includes

What Storm and Hurricane Damage Repair Covers in a Largo Property

Emergency Tarping and Board-Up

Roof breaches get tarped and mechanically fastened. Broken windows and doors are boarded to stop more water and to secure the property. This is the step that limits the loss, and insurers generally expect you to take it. Photos and measurements of the opening are recorded before it is covered.

Water Removal and Wet Material Demolition

Rain intrusion soaks insulation, drywall, ceiling texture and flooring along the path of the leak. We extract, open ceilings that are holding water, and remove saturated insulation and drywall so the cavity can dry. Attic insulation that is wet and compressed comes out rather than staying to hold moisture.

Structural Drying After Rain Intrusion

Wind-driven rain travels farther than owners expect, running along top plates and dropping into walls away from the entry point. Thermal imaging and meters find those paths, and the drying plan covers the whole run with daily readings, not just the room with the stain.

Interior Rebuild and Trade Coordination

Insulation, drywall, texture, paint, trim, ceilings and flooring go back once readings hold. Roofing, electrical and window work is coordinated with the appropriate licensed trades and permitted through the local building department when required.

Tell us the source and the flooring type and the crew arrives with the right equipment on the first trip.

How a Storm and Hurricane Damage Repair Job Runs, Step by Step

01

Safety Assessment

Downed lines, standing water near panels, sagging ceilings and compromised roof structure are identified before entry. Nothing gets touched until the site is safe.

02

Stabilize the Envelope

Tarping, board-up and temporary weather protection stop ongoing intrusion. We document the damage and the temporary measures with photos.

03

Remove Water and Wet Material

Extraction, ceiling openings, insulation removal and controlled demolition along the water path, with debris contained and hauled.

04

Equipment set by load, daily readings logged by room, and the water path mapped for the claim file.

05

Rebuild and Final Walk

Interior finishes restored, permitted trades coordinated, and a final walkthrough with the moisture log and photo record.

Local conditions

Storm and Hurricane Damage Repair and the Way Largo Buildings Are Built

Florida Building Code and Permits in Largo

Repairs in Pinellas County follow the Florida Building Code, which sets requirements for roof coverings, secondary water barriers, window and door products, and attachment. Replacing more than a defined portion of a roof covering triggers current code requirements rather than a simple patch. Product approvals matter for openings, and inspectors check them.

Permits for roofing, structural, electrical and mechanical work are issued by the City of Largo Building Division inside city limits and by Pinellas County Building Services for unincorporated addresses. Emergency tarping and drying do not wait on a permit, but the rebuild is scoped so the permitted trades can pull what they need.

Wind, Rain and Surge Are Three Different Claims

A homeowners policy typically responds to wind and to wind-driven rain that entered through a storm-created opening. Rising water and storm surge generally fall to a flood policy. When both happened in the same event, which is common near Cross Bayou and the coastal edges of the county, the file needs to show where each type of water went.

We record the height and pattern of the water, the location of the envelope breach, and the direction of travel inside the building. That evidence is far more useful two months later than a memory, and it is why we photograph before we cover an opening.

Signs You Need Storm and Hurricane Damage Repair Now

  • Ceiling stains or bulges appearing after a storm
  • Water running down the inside of an exterior wall
  • Lifted or missing shingles, or displaced roof tile
  • Wet or compressed attic insulation after heavy rain
  • Soffit or fascia damage with staining below it
  • Window or slider that leaked during high wind

Recognize two or more of these? Call and describe them. We will tell you what the readings usually mean.

Questions

Storm and Hurricane Damage Repair Questions From Largo Property Owners

Should I tarp the roof before calling my insurer?

Yes. Policies require you to prevent further damage. Photograph the damage first, then get it covered. Keep receipts and the photos, because both support the claim.

Can drying start while the roof is still open?

Only after the opening is covered. Running equipment under active intrusion wastes time and power. Tarping is usually done the same visit so drying can begin immediately after.

Does wind-driven rain count as flooding?

Generally no. Rain that entered through a storm-created opening is usually a wind claim, while rising water or surge falls under flood coverage. Because the handling differs, documenting the water's entry point is important.

Do you handle attic insulation after a roof leak?

Yes. Wet, matted insulation holds water against the ceiling drywall and reduces R-value permanently. We remove the affected area, dry the framing and decking, and include replacement in the rebuild scope.

How soon after a hurricane can you get to my property?

Availability is confirmed when you call. After a major storm, crews and materials across Pinellas County are in heavy demand, so getting on the schedule early and having your photos ready helps you move faster.

What should I photograph before cleanup starts?

Wide shots of each affected room, close-ups of the water line on the walls, the source if it is visible, and anything you have to move. Photograph before you remove or discard anything.

Does homeowners insurance cover hurricane damage in Florida?

Wind damage is generally covered, usually under a separate hurricane deductible. Rising water and storm surge are not, and need a flood policy. What the water did is less important than where it came from.

Can you board up before the insurance adjuster sees it?

Yes, and you should. Policies require you to prevent further damage. Board-up and tarping are emergency mitigation, and we document them with photographs for the claim.

How long does storm damage rebuild take?

Drying runs several days. The rebuild depends on materials and permitting, and after a large storm both are slower because the whole county is competing for the same supply and the same inspectors.

Do you handle tree impact damage to a roof?

We handle the water side, the board-up and the interior rebuild. Tree removal and structural framing repair are coordinated with the appropriate trade rather than done by our drying crews.
